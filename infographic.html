Family Tree of Information-Sharing Technologies

How today’s digital communication grew from earlier tools.

Roots

✍️

Writing Systems (~3200 BCE)

Recorded ideas could travel beyond memory.

Early writing systems allowed humans to preserve information instead of depending only on oral memory. This changed how knowledge could move across time and generations.
📜

Paper (~105 CE)

Information became lighter, cheaper, and easier to copy.

Paper made written communication more portable and practical. Over time, it became one of the most important materials for storing and distributing information.
🖨️

Movable Type & Printing Press

Mass reproduction accelerated access to knowledge.

Movable type and later Gutenberg's printing press helped make books and written information reproducible on a much larger scale. Printing changed education, religion, politics, and public access to knowledge.
Source: Project Gutenberg
📰

Newspapers (1600s)

Regular printed news created shared public conversations.

Newspapers made information more regular and public. People could increasingly encounter the same stories, political debates, and events at similar times.

Broadcast Era

⚡

Telegraph (1840s)

Messages moved faster than people.

The telegraph separated communication speed from transportation. Information could travel across long distances almost instantly compared with letters carried physically.
☎️

Telephone (1876)

Long-distance voice communication became immediate.

The telephone made real-time voice communication possible across distance and made communication more personal and direct.
📻

Radio

Broadcasting reached mass audiences at once.

Radio introduced one-to-many electronic communication. A single message, speech, or program could reach huge audiences.
Source: International Telecommunication Union
📺

Television

Sound and image reshaped public communication.

Television combined audio and moving images, creating a powerful new way to deliver entertainment, advertising, news, and political messages to mass audiences.

Network Era

💻

Internet

Networks connected computers across distance.

The internet brought previously separate communication technologies together into a global digital network.
Source: Internet Society
🌐

World Wide Web (1989–1991)

Information became linked, clickable, and easier to access.

The World Wide Web made internet information easier for ordinary users to navigate through websites and hyperlinks. It helped turn the internet into a public information environment.

Current Moment

📱

Smartphone

Communication became portable and constant.

Smartphones brought the internet, camera, telephone, messaging, maps, and countless other technologies together in one portable device.
💬

Social Media & Messaging

Anyone can publish, share, and react in real time.

Social media transformed audiences into participants. Instead of only receiving information, users can create, redistribute, comment on, and reshape it.
🤖

AI-Assisted Communication

Technology now helps create information as well as distribute it.

Generative AI represents another shift in information technology. Earlier tools mostly helped humans store, copy, or distribute information. AI can now participate in producing text, images, audio, and other content.

Why this matters now

Each new communication technology made information faster, more accessible, and more interactive. However, greater speed and access also create new problems involving trust, attention, misinformation, privacy, and influence.

Question to think about

If every generation creates a technology that makes information easier to produce and spread, does greater access to information necessarily make society better informed?
